Where the lines cross

Every line the app draws, rebuilt from the device's own recorded geometry and lane offsets (tools/replay/data/waybound-lanes-1789254859.json — 17 journeys, South Coast). Drawn with the project's own measurement convention (LaneHarness.ribbon, screenPointsPerMapPoint = 2), where one lane of 4.2 points spans 8.4 m of ground.

Images are generated artifacts (gitignored). Run python3 tools/lane-visualization/draw_lanes.py to produce real-lines.png and zoom windows. The script requires matplotlib only.

What this means

A crossing is a proper intersection of two drawn ribbons. An in-bundle crossing is one where both ribbons are stacked and well inside their shared run — an ordering artifact a subway-style corridor must not have, unlike a stub merge at a junction.

A lane-order rule does not move streets; it moves which lane each route occupies. The crossing counts CI measures answer: should routes 80 and 92 keep trading sides eleven times downtown, or should one hold its lane?
